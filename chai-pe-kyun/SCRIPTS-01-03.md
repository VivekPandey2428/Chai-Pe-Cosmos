# Chai Pe Kyun? Episodes 1–3 (scripts for review)

Format (from `SERIES.md`, "Series ideas"): one everyday sky "why?" per Short, about 25 s. Big question on screen at
frame 0, a one-line answer, one simple visual, one thing to try yourself, a comment question, the next question as a
teaser, and a loop. Inspector Sahab asks (curious, not issuing a challan); the Tapri wala answers. Same voices
(Ranga for Sahab, Bobby for the Tapri wala). Lines are in Roman Hinglish for review; they go to Devanagari for the voice
stage. **IS** = Inspector Sahab, **TW** = Tapri wala. Captions on screen stay in English.

Each episode chains to the next, so the first three play as a set: blue sky → red sunset (same physics, one step
further) → the Moon following you.

## Episode 1: Aasmaan neela kyun hai? (about 25 s)
**Frame-0 text:** "AASMAAN NEELA KYUN?" (big, centred)  ·  **Cover:** blue sky + "NEELA KYUN?" sticker

| ~Time | Who | Line | Picture |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.0 | IS | "Arey, yeh aasmaan neela kyun hai? Paint kisne kiya?" | Daytime home, both look up at a bright blue sky. Question text on screen. |
| 3.0 | TW | "Paint nahi sahab, dhoop! Dhoop mein saare rang chhupe hain." | A white sunbeam passes through a prism and fans into a rainbow. |
| 7.5 | TW | "Hawa ke chhote-chhote kan neele rang ko sabse zyada idhar-udhar bikher dete hain." | Whip up into the sky: tiny air balls bounce blue light in every direction while red and yellow fly straight through. |
| 13.0 | IS | "Toh poora aasmaan neele se bhar gaya!" | Wide: the bounced blue dots fill the whole dome. |
| 15.5 | TW | "Ghar pe try karo: paani mein do boond doodh, torch maaro, side se neela dikhega!" | A glass of milky water, a torch beam: blue glow from the side, orange where the beam comes out. Text: "GHAR PE TRY KARO". |
| 20.5 | IS | "Chaand pe aasmaan kis rang ka hoga? Comment karo!" | To camera; ~1 s subscribe callout after. |
| 23.5 | IS | "Agla sawaal... shaam ko suraj laal kyun?" | The sky warms toward sunset (the teaser). |
| 25.5 | TW | "Sahab, upar dekho..." | Loops to "Arey, yeh aasmaan neela kyun hai?" |

- **Comment answer (pinned):** black! The Moon has no air, so nothing scatters the light. Apollo photos show a black sky
  even in daytime.
- **Facts:** sunlight is a mix of all colours; air molecules scatter short wavelengths far more than long ones
  (Rayleigh scattering), so scattered blue reaches us from every direction. Violet scatters even more, but sunlight has
  less of it and our eyes are more sensitive to blue. (Not said on screen; for replies.)
- **Try at home is real:** milk droplets scatter blue sideways, so milky water looks bluish from the side and
  orange-ish looking along the beam.
- **New visuals:** a daytime sky with a prism + rainbow fan, air "balls" bouncing blue dots, and a glass of milky water
  with a torch beam.

## Episode 2: Shaam ko suraj laal kyun? (about 25 s)
**Frame-0 text:** "SURAJ LAAL KYUN?"  ·  **Cover:** a big orange setting Sun + "SHARMA RAHA HAI?" sticker

| ~Time | Who | Line | Picture |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.0 | IS | "Shaam ko suraj laal kyun ho jaata hai? Sharma raha hai kya?" | Sunset at home: a big orange Sun on the horizon. Question text on screen. |
| 3.5 | TW | "Sharma nahi raha sahab, lamba raasta!" | The Tapri wala laughs, points at the Sun. |
| 6.0 | TW | "Dopahar ko dhoop seedhi aati hai. Shaam ko tirchhi, hawa mein bahut lamba safar." | Whip to a side view of Earth with its thin air layer: a short beam straight down at noon, a long slanting beam at sunset. |
| 11.5 | TW | "Yaad hai, neela sabse zyada bikharta hai? Raaste mein neela kho jaata hai, laal-narangi bach jaata hai." | Along the long beam, blue dots peel away one by one; only orange and red reach the duo. |
| 17.5 | IS | "Matlab neela rang raaste mein hi utar gaya!" | Snap home; the cop nods, convinced. |
| 19.5 | IS | "Mars pe sunset kis rang ka hota hai? Comment karo!" | To camera; ~1 s subscribe callout. |
| 22.5 | IS | "Agla sawaal... chaand humare saath kyun chalta hai?" | Night falls; the Moon rises. |
| 24.5 | TW | "Sahab, kal subah phir dekhna..." | Loops. |

- **Comment answer (pinned):** blue! On Mars, fine dust sends blue light forward around the Sun, so Martian sunsets look
  bluish (NASA's Curiosity and Spirit rovers photographed this).
- **Facts:** near the horizon, sunlight crosses many times more air than at noon; the short (blue) wavelengths are
  scattered out along the way, leaving the reds and oranges. Dust and smoke make sunsets redder.
- **New visuals:** a sunset sky at home, and a side-on Earth with a glowing atmosphere layer and two beams (noon vs
  sunset). Reuses Episode 1's "blue dots peel away" effect.

## Episode 3: Chaand humare saath kyun chalta hai? (about 25 s)
**Frame-0 text:** "CHAAND PICHHA KAR RAHA HAI?!"  ·  **Cover:** the cop on a scooter, the Moon behind him +
"PICHHA KYUN?" sticker

| ~Time | Who | Line | Picture |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.0 | IS | "Main jahan jaata hoon, chaand peeche-peeche aata hai! Pichha kar raha hai kya?" | Night: the cop rides a little scooter around the tiny planet, glancing back at the Moon. Question text. |
| 4.0 | TW | "Pichha nahi sahab, chaand bahut door hai: lagbhag 3 lakh 84 hazaar kilometre!" | 3D number **3,84,000** km lands, label "KM DOOR". |
| 8.5 | TW | "Paas ke ped aur khambe tezi se peeche chhoot jaate hain..." | The scooter rides past trees and lamp posts that whoosh by. |
| 11.5 | TW | "...par itni door ki cheez ki disha badalti hi nahi." | Top-down diagram: the cop at three positions, sight lines to the faraway Moon all parallel; sight lines to a near tree swing wildly. |
| 16.0 | TW | "Aaj raat chalte hue dekho: ped bhaagega, chaand wahin rahega!" | Text: "AAJ RAAT TRY KARO". |
| 19.0 | IS | "Suraj bhi humare saath chalta hai kya? Comment karo!" | To camera; ~1 s subscribe callout. |
| 22.0 | IS | "Agla sawaal... taare tim-timate kyun hain?" | Stars twinkle overhead. |
| 24.0 | TW | "Sahab, chaand abhi bhi aapke peeche hai..." | The cop glances back: the Moon is still there. Loops. |

- **Comment answer (pinned):** yes, for the same reason. The Sun is even farther away (about 15 crore km), so it also
  seems to "follow" you.
- **Facts:** the Moon's average distance is about 384,400 km (NASA). Nearby objects shift a lot against the background
  as you move (parallax); very distant ones barely shift, so the Moon keeps the same direction and seems to keep pace.
- **New visuals:** a toy scooter, roadside trees and lamp posts, and a top-down sight-line diagram. The 3D number system
  already exists (and the "," glyph is in the font).

## Production notes
- **Voices:** about 7–8 lines per episode, roughly 250–300 credits each, about 850 for all three.
- **Length target:** 23–27 s. No silence before the first line; the question is on screen at frame 0.
- **Build:** the characters, home planet, tools/cycle2_patch.py and tools/cycle2_beats.py are reused. The beat
  generator needs one small change for this format: no trombone/kulhad/end-card beat (there is no challan). The
  "reversal" is replaced by the try-it-yourself beat.
- **Sources for the descriptions:** NASA Space Place "Why is the sky blue?"
  (https://spaceplace.nasa.gov/blue-sky/), NASA/JPL "Curiosity captures blue sunset on Mars" (May 2015; link to be checked
  before publishing), NASA Moon facts
  (https://science.nasa.gov/moon/facts/).
